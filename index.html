Every session

Daily

Before game 1 — warm-up (15–20 min)

  • Kovaak's, 10–12 min — 2–3 Viscose scenarios, not a full benchmark run
  • In-game DM, 5 min — headshot-only, chase clean hits not kills
  • Pick your agent before queueing — Sage or a Controller, decided before agent select

Between game 1 and game 2 (3–5 min)

  • Step away, breathe, stretch — no aim trainer reset
  • Name one specific death from the last game — not "played bad," the actual angle or read that was wrong

Can't name one? That's the flag you're already on autopilot — slow down before requeuing.

Hard stop rule Stop after 3 games, or immediately after 2 losses in a row under 1.0 K/D — whichever comes first. No exceptions for "one more."

After session

  • VOD review, 10–15 min — worst round only, watch positioning not just the death frame
  • Log agent / W-L + K/D / one mistake somewhere quick

Standing rule

Mindset

Judge decisions, not outcomes

Process over scoreline

A death from a well-timed aggressive read is a correct decision even if it loses the round. A survival from over-holding a passive angle can be a wrong decision even if it wins. Grade each round on whether the call made sense with the information you had — not on the kill feed.

"Was that a good read, or did it just work out?" — ask this after every death, win or lose.

Catch it before it compounds

Tilt tells

Your data already shows the pattern — a 0.9 K/D loss into a 0.4 K/D loss back to back (Sep 13→14). The behavioral tells usually show up before the scoreline does:

  • Forcing a duel to "make up for" your last death, instead of playing the round in front of you
  • Holding a grudge against one specific enemy across rounds
  • Typing in chat instead of calling in voice/comms

Any one of these mid-game is a cue to use the hard stop rule early, not wait for game 3.

Separate identity from role fit

Reframe Duelist losses

Your Controller and Initiator win rates (50.4%, 50.7%) beat Duelist (44.9%) by a wide margin. A rough game on Reyna isn't evidence you're bad — it's evidence you picked a role that statistically doesn't fit your playstyle as well. Losing on the wrong tool for the job is a pick problem, not a you problem.

Reduce variance, not just raise the ceiling

Pre-round routine as an anchor

MVP games (Sep 7, Aug 23, Aug 22) sitting next to sub-1.0 K/D losses means your ceiling is fine — your floor is the problem. A fixed pre-round routine (same crosshair check, same breath, same "what's my role this round" question) keeps baseline execution steady regardless of how the last round went, which raises the floor more than any single mechanical fix will.

Autopilot habits

  • Say your round plan out loud before it starts — forces active decisions over muscle memory
  • Stand up, water, look away — every 2 games, no exceptions
  • Vary the warm-up scenario order occasionally so the warm-up itself doesn't go on autopilot
